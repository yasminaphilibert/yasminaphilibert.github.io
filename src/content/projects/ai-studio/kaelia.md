A dress that started as a photograph of something that did not exist.

The first two frames came out of a single prompt: a long turtleneck gown, tie-dye burst crossed with leopard, worn on a black lava field under a flat white sky. Everything after that was the unglamorous half of fashion — turning a picture into a garment. The print was rebuilt as a seamless repeat with no offset, the gown was unwrapped into flat pattern pieces with seam allowance, and the repeat was laid onto a 160 cm roll to prove five dresses could be cut from it.

Kaelia is the woman the clothes were cut for. Raised among volcanic landscapes, reserved, deliberate, more at ease with weather than with people. She exists as a model sheet — turnaround, expressions, palette, scale reference — so the campaign could be shot again and again without her changing.

The campaign was finished a year after it started, and it runs in both colorways on the same black field: the navy the dress was born in, and the burnt orange it was engineered for. Nothing here was photographed. Everything here was cut, printed, and shot.
